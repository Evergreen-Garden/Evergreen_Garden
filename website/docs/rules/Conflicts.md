# Conflicts

Conflicts are any form of violent interaction between animals.
The most important rules of any conflict are:

- Do not interrupt an ongoing conflict unless specifically stated in your animal’s profile;
- Each species has its own simple rules on what they can and cannot attack, so read the profiles for that information;
- Immature animals count as belonging to **small** size category for hunting purposes, regardless of species;
- Unless stated otherwise, if any one participant of the conflict dies, the fight ends. This is known as the “*body down*” rule;
- A notable exception from the body down rule are immature animals and small-sized animals that accidentally die when the intended target was their larger group member. They are regarded as “collateral damage”, and their bodies can be ignored in the pursuit of the intended target;
- If your conflict got interrupted by a rulebreaking player, both parties can team up to kill the offender and can choose to stop the fight or resume it after the intruder has been dealt with;
- *White crow effect* a.k.a. *black sheep effect*: visibly pure albino and melanistic animals can be hunted, challenged and even cannibalised by anyone regardless of size category and species. Such unusual pigmentation makes survival hard, and only the most tough animals can afford living with albinism or melanism.;
- A group of same-sized animals counts as one size category above what they would count as individually. For example, a group of *huge* dinosaurs can be considered a *giant* target for hunting purposes, and be targeted by predators that would not be normally able to go for *huge* animals;
- “*Survivor’s Grace*”: please wait at least *2 minutes* before engaging in conflict with a party that you witnessed leaving another fight **or** logging in. You should not jump in to kill off the survivor right away!
- Grouping up during combat is not allowed, and any additional group members cannot join the fight after 2 minutes!
- Sleeping during any conflicts is strictly prohibited. If your dinosaur lays down to sleep, the conflict is considered over, and you must forfeit. Adrenaline prevents the nervous system from resting!
- All combat stops at 1 minute before server restart.
 
There are 3 main types of conflicts important to the rules:

## 1. Hunts

A hunt is a form of conflict between predator and prey. There is always an attacking side and a defending side. Most of the time only carnivores can hunt, with only a few exceptions.

- A carnivore can initiate a hunt if their hunger is roughly below 75% (top of the stomach icon);
- A carnivore can initiate a hunt however they want, be it a silent ambush or an obvious rush;
- An ongoing hunt should not be interrupted by a third party unless specifically stated in the profile;
- A hunt ends if any participant drops dead, if not stated otherwise. All combat stops after a body is down. There are a few exceptions to this rule, however;
- After making a kill, the hunting party should fill up and satisfy their hunger. Leaving the body without filling the stomach is not allowed!
- A carnivore is protected from being hunted as long as they have a body with them. They can use this time to rest and heal. However, carnivores can choose to leave the protection of the body once they fill up on food completely and the body is decayed by at least a **50%** (except *colossal* bodies);
- If a herbivore manages to kill their attacker, they may also claim the body to stay with it and receive protection from any conflicts and use this time to heal in peace. They may leave the body at any time;
- Carnivores can be contested for their kill, and for that use *contests and challnges* rules;
- Carnivores have full right to defend their kill against unwanted scavengers without warning;
- Carnivores can only eat their fallen group-mates if they are indicated as cannibals that can eat dead bodies of the same species and the body is not claimed;
- Carnivores cannot contest the body of their fallen packmate;
- You must wait at least 10 minutes before hunting the same party a second time;
- While the attacking side always hunts with lethal intent, the defending side doesn’t have to kill the attacker, sometimes chasing a predator off is enough.

## 2. Contests and challenges
Contests are a form of conflict that involves two parties fighting over space or resources outside of hunting needs. Any animal has a right to be aggressive and defend its space, but some creatures are more aggressive than others, having the temper to challenge others without much reasoning.

- Contests can be fought for territory (a specific location) or resources (a body of water, a bush or a carcass), just over personal space, or, for some species, just for the thrill of it;
- All carnivores can challenge other carnivores for a kill within their profile rules;
- Herbivores can challenge both carnivores and herbivores to a fight;
- Contests should always be initiated by aggressive calls and bluff attacks to clearly show intent;
- Contests can be initiated on any level of hunger;
- If not stated otherwise, an animal can usually contest any other animal that fits the same size category or below;
- When invited for a contest, an opponent can immediately surrender by making alert calls and fleeing. They should leave the resources behind and clearly show that they aren’t interested in a fight;
- If an opponent answers with aggressive calls, the contest can begin. It should not be interrupted, and the fighting parties cannot be hunted;
- If the opponent ignores your invitation for a contest, you should make sure that they aren’t AFK. If they are fully aware of your presence and still ignore aggression, you can attack;
- Although contests can end up in death of one of the combatants, one of the parties has a right to end the contest by fleeing and making alert calls. This means surrender and should be respected by the winner;
- If any one of the participating combatants dies, this automatically ends the contest. Carnivores and omnivores can eat from a fallen opponent if it’s not of the same species (with the exception of full cannibals), and it grants them the same protection as if it was a hunt;
- Herbivores can also claim bodies.
- If you witnessed a body down, you must wait 2 minutes before contesting (with the exception of corpse pirates);
- Herbivores can be contested off bodies by carnivores and have a right to defend the carcass and not allow the carnivore to feed;
- *Scavengers* can eat or steal from claimed bodies as long as the owner isn’t actively eating- that includes trophies as well!

## 3. Duels
Duels, also known as ritualistic combat, are intraspecific sparring matches between two members of one group. While winning a duel doesn’t yield any practical gameplay benefits, they are a fun way to spend time and practice battle prowess. Optionally, you can choose to pass around group leadership through duels.

- To initiate a duel, an animal needs to clearly invite the opponent with aggressive calls and bluff attacks;
- The second party can choose to pass by not showing aggression and making alert calls, or accept the duel and answer with aggressive calls of their own. This starts a duel;
- When a duel is started, all other group members should move back and give combatants space;
- Duels should never have lethal intent. As both combatants see each-other’s health, make sure not to kill your opponent. Duels emphasise showing off power and fitness with performative showy moves and roars over pure killing efficiency;
- Duels should not be interrupted by other group members and are always done 1v1, but a lurking carnivore has a full right to attack duelling animals to catch them off guard;
- A duel ends if one of the opponents gets low on health or surrenders with alert calls.
