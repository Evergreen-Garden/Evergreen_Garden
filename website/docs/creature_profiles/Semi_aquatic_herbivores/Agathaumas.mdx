# Agathaumas

## 🔎 Known species
- *A. sylvestris*

🐃 **Size category**: huge;
🕑 **Passive growth time**: 3 hours;
🔷 **Group size**: 1-3

## 🍽️ Diets
- **Waterfront Specialist**: lakeweed, reeds, kelp, leaves, nuts, melons, roots;
- **Seafarer**: mollusks, kelp, fish, lakeweed, reeds.


## 🇺🇸 Speculative evolution
When Edward Drinker Cope first described *Agathaumas sylvestris* in the late 19th century, the animal was known only from a collection of non-diagnostic bones from the Late Cretaceous of Wyoming, US. Later, workers would encounter more dinosaurs with similar bones that would be named *Ceratopsians*.
The reconstruction that followed, most famously illustrated by Charles R. Knight, presented a heavily-built horned dinosaur that inhabited river valleys. But as paleontology moved away from the belief that dinosaurs were swamp-bound reptiles, this view faded from the scientific discourse, remaining only in art. The genus of *Agathaumas*, named so very early in the timeline of ceratopsian research, has been declared dubious, since it is impossible to identify the relationships of this animal based on the available material.
Yet in the waterways of the Garden, *Agathaumas* is real, and it is unlike any other known horned dinosaur.
With 3 horns, *Agathaumas* is a member of the *Triceratopsini* tribe, and a close relative of animals such as *Triceratops*, *Torosaurus*, *Eotriceratops* and *Titanoceratops*. Like them, it is a massive large-bodied ceratopsid with a square-shaped mostly solid frill, as well as a pair of great brow horns. Unlike its relatives, *Agathaumas* has an unusually developed nasal horn that exceeds even the brow horns in size. Its skull is also ornamented with enlarged epiparietal spikes that usually get absorbed into the frill bone in mature *Triceratopsini*.
The differences don’t end with the skull: *Agathaumas* postcranial morphology shows distinct adaptations for traversing soft muddy soil, such as flattened digit bones and a more stocky graviportal frame. Its bones are also a lot denser, allowing *Agathaumas* to sink to the bottom when swimming.


## In the Garden
Stomp and splash! Make way for the great wonder!
Emerging from silt and mud, *Agathaumas* is a force of nature to behold. With giant horns and stomping hooves, these herbivores unceremoniously uproot reeds, break trees, excavate new pools and reshape the landscape in their path. Just one is enough to leave behind a trail of devastation - a whole herd may completely change the environment.
However, these destructive habits create space for new life: the presence of *Agathaumas* engineers a unique type of wetland ecosystem.
The strange ceratopsid is always found near water: from pristine coastal shallows to the most muddy of swamps. Covered in dirt, pondweed and salt, they resemble moving piles of rubble and jagged driftwood.
Clashes with waterside predators are frequent, as *Agathaumas* have to protect their young from crocodylomorphs and spinosaurs.


## 🪷 Habitat
- **Wetland**: *Agathaumas* can be found in any type of swamp: bogs, marshlands, mangroves, mires, mudflats, you name it!
- **Waterside**: some populations distinctly prefer still freshwater habitats, feeding on reeds and lakeweeds;
- **Coastal**: some populations prefer the salty wash of the ocean and dive under the cold waves to graze on kelp.

## 🌿 Roleplaying guidelines
- **Sexual dimorphism**: most of the time females exhibit a distinct phenotype with smaller horns and rounder frill, while males carry monstrously large ornaments. This is not a hard rule, but merely a statistic: there are some bulls that resemble females, and there are cows that resemble males;
- The shape of horns and frill spikes is subject to great variability in the genus. The different shapes may occur due to genetics, life history or developmental trauma;
- Aggression and dominance don’t correlate to the size of the horns: even a bull with short stumpy horns can be scrappy!
- *Agathaumas* are heavy and lazy creatures that take lots of rest. Between sessions of feeding they often relax on the shore, wallow in mud or passively float in water;
- Most often feed during night time, while spending the daylight hours soaking themselves in water and snoozing;
- *Agathaumas* possesses specialised kidneys that let it tolerate high salinity and poor quality water;
- When out of water, often cover their skin in a layer of mud to protect it from drying out;
- Could never resist pushing down decaying trees;
- *Agathaumas* are stubborn creatures that are not easily scared;
- *Agathaumas* often try to intimidate and scare off an opponent first before going into battle: with such massive horns, fights can lead to brutal injuries, and combat should serve as a last resort.


## 🩵 Grouping
- Can have up to 3 individuals in a group, called a wallow or a stomping;
- A group of *Agathaumas* counts as a *giant* target;
- Immature individuals don’t count towards the group limit;
- Grouped members can all defend each-other if attacked.

## 🧩 Mixed-species grouping
- Can group with any *small*-sized herbivores;
- **Ceratopsian Crusade**: can group with *Pachycephalosaurus*, *Styracosaurus*, *Albertaceratops*, *Pachyrhinosaurus* and *Eotriceratops*;
- **Wetland Crew**: can group with *Lurdusaurus*, *Desmatosuchus* and *Deinocheirus*;
- Can include 1 deviant *Troubadour Lambeosaurus* in a group;
- Can include 1 deviant *Ringer Muttaburrasaurus* in a group;
- A lone *Agathaumas* may group with any *small* or *medium* herbivores (see deviant profile).

## 🪺 Reproduction
- **Nest materials**: reeds, branches, roots
- Prefer to make nests on muddy soil;
- Young *Agathaumas* are called bucks or calves;
- *Agathaumas* are ferociously protective parents, and go into a berserker frenzy if their offspring are in danger;
- Mature quickly, but growth gradually slows down at later life stages.


## 🌳 Claiming food
- *Agathaumas* specialise on foraging aquatic vegetation and cannot claim berry bushes.

## 🚩 Claiming territory
- An *Agathaumas* may choose to claim a body of water as its *waterhole*;
- The *waterhole* must be at least deep enough to swim in to be claimed as territory;
- While moving around in its *waterhole*, *Agathaumas* may challenge any semi-aquatic or aquatic creatures to leave the body of water;
- Additionally, it can challenge any terrestrial creature that comes too close to the shoreline, however, it **does not** pursue them inland once they choose to leave;
- *Agathaumas* **cannot** challenge *small*-sized creatures and ignores them completely;
- *Agathaumas* are highly intolerant of water-dwelling predators and often immediately challenge any aquatic or semi-aquatic carnivores that appear in their claimed area;
- The aggression of *Agathaumas* does not correlate to water quality, as these ceratopsians are entirely not concerned about pollutants and can inhabit even the most disgusting dirty watering holes. The biggest factor in *Agathaumas* territorial aggression is predator exclusion, as many calves are taken by crocodiles;
- *Agathaumas* cannot claim waterholes when terrestrial species are present in its group.


## 👹 Deviant profile *Bog Sage*
- As *Agathaumas* individuals age, they become more and more sedentary with time, rarely moving from their favourite wallow;
- Such individuals, often called the *Bog Sages*, have a dominating presence that often pacifies the destructive impulses of the younger *Agathaumas*. And if they don’t listen to reason, they will listen to the horn and hoof;
- The *Bog Sages* are old and wise, their youthful fire spent, giving way to peaceful contentment. However, one would be a fool to mistake their peaceful restfulness for weakness: even past their prime, the *Bog Sages* are fierce warriors that defend the serenity of their home swamp;
- A *Bog Sage* cannot group with other *Agathaumas*;
- A *Bog Sage* can freely group with **any** *small* and *medium* herbivores, serving as protector of the meek creatures that come to rest by the pond;
- A *Bog Sage* should stay within its home pond or swamp to enact this profile;
- A home pond can be any piece of freshwater wide enough to house the *Bog Sage*. The depth does not matter;
- The *Bog Sage* defends its flock from any danger as long as they stay by the water. The flock may help their Sage in any conflicts around the pond;
- The *Bog Sage* may challenge other *Agathaumas* to a duel if it decides that they upset the order of the swamp and cause ruckus;
- *Bog Sages* often choose old overgrown bogs as their home, often inhabiting hollow decaying tree trunks;
- *Bog Sages* usually have huge, gnarly and damaged horns indicative of their vast age.


## ☂️ Survival tips
- You can use water to escape the dangers of land;
- Don’t get caught in the open! *Agathaumas* is slow and cumbersome on dry land;
- *Agathaumas* can feed on a variety of waterside plants and can exploit unusual feeding resources;
- *Agathaumas* can passively absorb water through osmosis while swimming, be it saltwater or even 0% quality freshwater;
- You can choose between abilities that work better on if you’re dry or if you’re wet;
- Common nicknames: aga, agatha;
- Survival difficulty: ⭐️⭐️⭐️(moderately challenging)
