# Lurdusaurus

## 🔎 Known species
- *L. arenatus*;

**🪿 Size category**: medium;
**🌾 Diet**: shrubs, fruit, ground vegetation, lakeweed, kelp, reeds;
**🐚 Supplementary food**: mollusks;
**🌐 Range**: Gondwa, Beringia, Aquatica.

## 🇳🇪 In real life
*Lurdusaurus* was an unusual iguanodontian dinosaur known from the Early Cretaceous of Niger. It possessed a stocky rotund build and had unusually dense bones, which has lead to speculations that it was adapted for a semiaquatic lifestyle in the wetlands. Now we know that *Lurdusaurus* was probably not a semi-aquatic animal in reality. The forelimbs of *Lurdusaurus* were armed with massive thumb spikes.
It shared its habitat with other wetland creatures such as *Suchomimus* and *Sarcosuchus*.

## In the Garden
In the Garden, *Lurdusaurus* is a unique amphibious herbivore. Spending a large portion of its life in bodies of water and feeding on water plants and algae, it successfully avoids competition with any of the terrestrial plant-eaters. These thick-bellied dinosaurs can be seen lounging on muddy and sandy banks, galumphing on the beach in an awkward trot, but in the water they transform. Despite its mass, *Lurdusaurus* is an agile swimmer capable of holding its breath for long periods of time to feed on the bottom.
It is a herbivore unlike any other, a bizarre master of two worlds.


## 🪷 Habitat
- Are commonly found anywhere in and around water: from small desert oasises to lakes and marshlands, to marine waters and tidal flats;
- Build their nests anywhere near water, be it on riverbanks, on the seashore or on distant islands away from dangers of the mainland;
- Colonial nesters: a herd can build a vast nesting colony along the shoreline;
- Some individuals lead a fully terrestrial lifestyle;

## 🦎 Roleplaying guidelines
- Sexual dimorphism: males commonly sport a row of dermal spikes along the back, while females are more likely to be smooth. This is not a hard rule, but merely a statistical trend. Males also tend to be more aggressive;
- Often lazily relax on the beach when not hungry, wallowing in the mud, honking and bellowing;
- Exhibit breaching behaviour to dislodge skin parasites;
- Mostly forage and travel on land when the sun is down;
- Prefer to stay near water during the day;
- Run around and play in the mud on rainy days, greatly enjoying the downpour;
- Readily consume several species of kelp and seaweed from both freshwater and marine habitats. Some individuals are even known to have serrated beaks to more easily mow down the macroalgae;
- Marine populations are adapted for drinking saline water and excrete excess salt through glands located in their noses;
- On rare occasions a herd can suddenly abandon their beach and migrate onto another spot, sometimes on the other side of the island. Other beaches are used for generations;
- Highly enjoy the company of *Hibbertopterus* and their cleaning services!


## 🩵 Grouping
- Can have up to 6 mature individuals in a group, called a slide, a colony or a galumph;
- Can have as many immature individuals as fit in a group, called dumplings;
- The leader of a colony is called a “beachmaster”.

## 🧩 Mixed grouping
- Can group with any herbivores.

## ⚡ Aggression
- *Lurdusaurus* can challenge animals of medium, large and huge sizes;
- A slide can claim a stretch of the shoreline or a pond and exhibit territorial aggression towards unwanted intruders;
- Members of a colony often aggressively duel one-another in violent clashes. Males tend to be more inclined to engage in pointless violence and often carry countless scars from thumb spikes across their sides. All for the title of the beachmaster!

## ☂️ Survival tips
- You can survive entirely off seawater by sneezing out the salt;
- You can also sneeze out any negative status effects like blood and venom;
- “*Mudslide*” transfers the “wet” condition onto your target, leaving you dry;
- You can dodge predators of both land and water by using your amphibious nature;
- *Lurdusaurus* does not take fall damage very well.
- Common nickname: lurdu;
- Survival difficulty: ⭐️⭐️ (not very hard);
