# Apatosaurus

## 🔎 Known species:
- *A. louisae*;
- *A. ajax*;

🦕 **Size category**: colossal;
🌿 **Diet**: shrubs, fruit, ground vegetation;
🦴 **Supplementary food**: bones, mollusks;


## 🇺🇸 In real life
*Apatosaurus* was a massive long-necked dinosaur from the Late Jurassic of North America. This iconic sauropod from the Morrison Formation is notable for having highly robust proportions compared to its peers, with wide limb bones and an enormously thick neck. Its tail was long and thin like a whip, a defining feature of the *Flagellicaudata* clade to which it belonged. It fed on ground level, swallowing vegetation into its enormous gut without chewing. Sauropods like *Apatosaurus* had an expansive system of air-sacks inside their bodies that helped them with efficient breathing and to conserve weight.
This behemoth shared its habitat with a whole parade of sauropod relatives and other iconic dinosaurs such as *Allosaurus* and *Stegosaurus*.

## In the Garden
In the Garden, *Apatosaurus* is a not-so-gentle giant. The bony studs on its burly neck and the bullwhip-tail are not just for show, and *Apatosaurus* is a gladiator among sauropods. When giants rumble, the earth thunders!
Though not exceptionally large by sauropod standards, *Apatosaurus* still dwarfs most inhabitants of the Garden. Giant fortresses of flesh, they stomp around the fields and meadows, shaking the hills with songs and mighty bellows. *Apatosaurus* is an active, curious and bellicose animal and not just a living peace of scenery or a passive dim-witted plant-eater. *Apatosaurus* is an epic beast!

## ⛰️ Habitat
- Prefer to nest on the forest edge, finding a quiet clearing to dig a simple nest and lay eggs;
- Colonial nesters: many *Apatosaurus* may lay eggs close to each-other to overwhelm the predators with the amount of offspring;
- Upon hatching, the young try to band together and disappear into the forest. For now, hiding is their best hope at survival;
- Mature *Apatosaurus* dwell in more open habitats like plains, scrublands and sparse forests.


## 🦎 Roleplaying guidelines
- Sexual dimorphism: on average, males tend to have a ridge of dermal spikes along their backs, while females tend to be smooth. This is not a hard rule, but just a statistical trend;
- Aloof parents, but may still defend youngsters;
- Bulk browsers: consume all vegetation in their path. Swallow first, think second;
- Have a bizarre habit of chewing on bones to replenish calcium: sometimes even the bones of other *Apatosaurus*…
- Often search for gastroliths among shellfish clusters;
- Have bony knobs along their necks, which are involved mainly in intraspecific combat. The lash-like tail is used as a defensive weapon against predators and is rarely raised against fellow *Apatosaurus*;
- Can crack their tails in the air as a warning before aggression;
- Sing, bellow and rumble: *Apatosaurus* are very vocal animals that communicate over long distances and greet each-other upon meeting. Their complex air sack system makes their whole body into a giant bagpipe;
- Perform a mating dance by slowly arching their necks up and down, mirroring one-another;
- Some ridgeback males don’t tolerate other ridgeback males in their territory and contest them on sight; the rarer smooth-back males, however, infiltrate unnoticed. The females do not show preference bias for either group;
- Most *Apatosaurus* are polygynandious, having no long-term mating partners, however, they are known to form friendships;
- *Apatosaurus* get easily irritated and chase off annoying animals;
- Adolescent *Apatosaurus* sometimes chase around and torment other animals seemingly for fun, growing out of their juvenile skittishness and acquiring newfound bravado. This time in their life is also when many of them get killed, as they still have a long way before losing their youthful stupidity;
- Love to curiously inspect and sniff unusual small animals they find;


## 💚 Grouping
- Can have up to 2 mature individuals in a group, called a pair;
- Can have as many immature individuals as fit in a group, called sauropodlets;
- A group of sauropodlets is called a *creche*;
- Adults sometimes may congregate into a herd called a *quake* or a *thunder*. Many apatosaurus can move and forage together in a *quake*, but they can’t defend ungrouped individuals and simply move away if another group gets attacked.

## 🧩 Mixed grouping
- May group with any herbivores.

## ⚡ Aggression
- Can challenge animals of giant size and above;
- Readily whack any unwelcome animals in their personal space;
- Members of a quake often duel one-another using neck slaps. Such battle is called a brontosmash!

## 🪦 Other interactions
- A dead *Apatosaurus* counts as a *Whalefall* and cannot be claimed by any one party. All carnivores may freely share the dead sauropod, and no conflicts can start on a whalefall.


## 👹 Deviant profile *the Swamp Master*
- Some *Apatosurus* individuals enjoy water more than dry land, spending their time wading through lakes;
- Such specimens are known as the *Swamp Masters* and attract a whole pool party of wetland herbivores to their side;
- A *Swamp Master* can claim a lake and if alone, may defend it from intruders of *giant* size and above without warning;
- Additionally, a lone *Swamp Master* may interfere in ongoing conflicts at the edge of its lake, but may attack only the party of *giant* size and above;
- There can be only one *Swamp Master* in a group;
- A *Swamp Master* can only group with semi-aquatic herbivores, but loses __both__ the right to attack without warning and to 3-rd party while being grouped. Instead, it must challenge other animals in a normal way;
- A lake can be home for only 1 *Swamp Master*, and it __must__ challenge any other *Apatosaurus* that wants to claim their lake as a *Swamp Master*;
- *Swamp Masters* are known to heavily supplement their diet with freshwater mollusks, making them a final host for some of the largest parasitic worms known to science;
- Swallow lots of gastroliths they find among the bivalves;
- It is currently unknown why *Apatosaurus* become Swamp Masters, however it is theorised that this lifestyle is a way to treat bone sickness by suspending the colossal weight of the animal in the water and reducing the stress on the bones. This lines up with the consumption of shellfish for calcium and protein needed for bone growth;
- At leats some of the Swamp Mistresses were found out to be gravid females looking for rest and a source of calcium;
- *Swamp Masters* enjoy *Hibbertopterus* cleaning their skin!


## ☂️ Survival tips
- Crouching makes you lower your head to the ground and raise your tail to hit targets of unconventional hight;
- Your whip-like tail is your main weapon! Keep it facing towards your opponents!
- Like all sauropodlets, juvenile *Apatosaurus* are slow and defenceless;
- The only things that can pose a threat to an adult *Apatosaurus* are pairs of giant carcharodontosaurs, mobs of huge carnivores and… other *Apatosaurus*;
- Common nicknames: apa, apato;
- Survival difficulty: ⭐️⭐️(not very hard)
