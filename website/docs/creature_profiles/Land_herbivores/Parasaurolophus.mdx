# Parasaurolophus

## 🔎 Known species
- *P. walkeri*;
- *P. cyrtocristatus*;
- *P. tubicen*;
- *P. argonaut*;
- *P. campionem* (possibly just an elder morph of *P. walkeri*);

🐘 **Size category**: giant;
🌿 **Diet**: shrubs, fruit, roots;
🐚 **Supplementary food**: mollusks;

## 🇨🇦 In real life
*Parasaurolophus* is an iconic member of the Late Cretaceous North American fauna. This animal ranged throughout all latitudes of the continent, being represented by several known species. It is theorised that their long tubular crests were used as a sound amplifier, producing loud and possibly complex calls. Like all hadrosaurs, it was an advanced herbivore with complex teeth batteries for chewing vegetation - an apparatus that has not been surpassed by any animals since. It was also a facultative biped, being able to walk both on 2 or on 4 legs. Like its relatives, *Parasaurolophus* appears to have exhibited parental care.
In spite of previous theories, it appears that the short-crested *P. cyrtocristatus*  is not a female morph, but a distinct species.

## In the Garden
On the fields and meadows of the Garden, *Parasaurolophus* is a striking and majestic creature. Both mighty and elegant, this musical lambeosaurine is a keystone herbivore of the grasslands, and many smaller plant-eaters flock to their melodies for safety. *Parasaurolophus* is the most social out of the giant herbivores of the Garden, forming vast herds with complex hierarchies, where individuals of different age and experience assume different roles. Although they have a gentle character, *Parasaurolophus* are no strangers to violence, and are ready to rain down their mighty hooves upon anyone who threatens the safety of their family… Or dares to get disgusting dirt on their shiny crest!
A glamorous and flamboyant singer, *Parasaurolophus* has all the rights to be a show-off, and The whole of the Garden is its stage.


## ⛰️ Habitat
- Roam and nest among the vast open grass fields and in sparse woodland;
- Colonial nesters: herds often use common nesting fields and employ a net of predator detection through neighbours. Many eyes make raising young safe!

## 🦎 Roleplaying guidelines
- Love to playfully prance around the fields on two legs;
- Rear up on two legs to greet one-another and for a courting waltz;
- Use bipedal gait to observe their surroundings and when slowly roaming: *Parasaurolophus* likes to turn its nose up, away from the plebeian crowd;
- Loud, proud and very talkative;
- Herd members try to always stay in contact using calls and body language;
- Very tender and protective parents;
- Picky eaters, preferring to browse on young shrubbery, but readily supplemented their diet with nutritious roots and even protein-rich mollusks when visiting coasts!
- While being welcoming and indifferently calm towards herbivores and even some carnivores, herds of *Parasaurolophus* react quite strongly to the presence of *giant* carnivores. They angrily trumpet their war songs and stomp their hooves, and an injured or cornered *Parasaurolophus* becomes enraged, kicking away with frenzied bloodlust. *Parasaurolophus* is capable of being both a beast of peace and a beast of war;
- Express disapproval and discomfort by huffing and sneezing at animals that bother them;
- Often practice alloparenting, with family members helping with raising young;
- There is a significant percentage of *Parasaurolophus* individuals that prefer their own sex as partners and that exhibit behavior atypical for their birth sex. Even if such individuals never contribute to producing offspring, they make for excellent caretakers of the herd’s young! This is especially true considering that they are typically free of the hierarchical competition;
- Love the company of hadrosaurs and ceratopsids.


## 💚 Grouping
- Can have up to 2 mature individuals in a group, called a pair;
- A pair of *Parasaurolophus* counts as a *colossal* target;
- Can have as many immature individuals as fit in a group, called foals;
- Are very social, and several pairs and families may come together as a *symphony* or a *parade*. A symphony is a herd where members socialise, duel and roam together. Once predators strike, only grouped members are allowed to defend one-another, and others typically run away in distress;
- A symphony is lead by a single dominant individual, called a *helicon*.

## 🧩 Mixed grouping
- May group with any herbivores.

## ⚡ Aggression
- Can challenge animals of huge, giant and colossal size;
- Sometimes members of a symphony will violently duel for the position of a *helicon*, especially stallions;
- May interrupt an ongoing conflict as a 3-rd party *specifically* to save ungrouped immature parentless foals they come across.


## ☂️ Survival tips
- Facultative biped: instead of crouching, *Parasaurolophus* assumes a two-legged stance;
- Reared up on hind legs and using front kicks you are able to outdamage all of the giant carnivores in a face-to-face battle;
- You are generally fast enough to get away from most giant carnivores;
- Use your calls: they provide great utility!
- Common nickname: para;
- Survival difficulty: ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️(challenging)
