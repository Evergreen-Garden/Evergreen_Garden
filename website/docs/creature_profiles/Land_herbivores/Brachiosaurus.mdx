# Brachiosaurus

## 🔎 Known species:
- *B. altithorax*;

## 🔍 Related genera:
- *Giraffatitan brancai*;
- *Sauroposeidon proteles*.

🦕 **Size category**: Colossal 
🕑 **Passive growth time**: 4 hours
💟 **Total group limit**: 2 mature individuals 

## 🍽️ Diet
- **Landscaper**: whatever it can fit in its mouth.


## 🇺🇸 In real life
*Brachiosaurus* is a tall and towering sauropod dinosaur. Hailing from the Morrison Formation of the Late Jurassic North America, it is a celebrity among ancient reptiles. While many of its contemporaries had more horizontal postures, *Brachiosaurus* reached for the skies to tower above all. It was a specialised high browser, feeding from the highest treetops. It was the largest creature in its environment. *Brachiosaurus* lived side by side with other iconic Jurassic sauropods such as *Apatosaurus*, *Diplodocus* and *Camarasaurus*.
*Giraffatitan* was a close relative that lived in what is now Tanzania. Most of the popular depictions of *Brachiosaurus* are actually based on *Giraffatitan*, as it was historically known from more complete remains.

## In the Garden
Slowly moving through the conifer scrubland, being as tall as the trees themselves, *Brachiosaurus* inspires awe and wonder. An intense presence follows the towering colossus: herbivores and carnivores alike make way before its earth-shaking stride.
This giant was not born this way, surviving, feeding and growing for decades in tall brush and fern, until one day it was left without enemies to fear save for the largest of carnivores. It became a beacon for smaller creatures that follow in its shadow. Small pterosaurs use its vast sides to perch, while little herbivores hop around underneath its legs.
For rivals and enemies *Brachiosaurus* is a dreadful foe. For small and meek - a tower of hope.


## ⛰️ Habitat preference
- **Scrubland**: sparse conifer groves are the preferred habitat for *Brachiosaurus*;
- **Desert**: *Brachiosaurus* is a hardy animal and can survive long migrations across dry environments, decimating any oasis it arrives at;
- **Woodland**: *Brachiosauruses* often enter open forests to feast upon the fresh canopy leaves.

## 🌿 Roleplaying guidelines
- **Sexual dimorphism**: males often sport a characteristic row of dorsal spikes on top of their necks, as well as heavy rough scutes protecting their front legs during intraspecific combat, while females are predominantly smooth. This is not a hard rule, but merely a statistic: a *Brachiosaurus* can develop or not develop spikes regardless of sex;
- Often hauntingly call into the distance, broadcasting their territory;
- Love to take baths in lakes and oases to wash off the dust;
- Often consume rocks to turn then into gastroliths - stomach stones that aid with grinding up plant matter;
- Often inspect unfamiliar objects by taste;
- Feed almost constantly to support their enormous bulk;
- Generally lead solitary lives and keep distance from other giant sauropods. Herds are uncommon: *Brachiosaurus* prefers solitude;
- In contrast, perfectly tolerate the company of smaller creatures, who often follow *Brachiosaurus* around for protection;
- Spiked males often clash with one-another for territory and dominance, rearing up and punching away. Such battles shake the ground;
- Courting is done by performing long melancholic songs. These giants seek not strength or size, but a perfect voice to complete the duet;
- Rare individuals survive to an ancient age.


## 💚 Grouping
- A group can consist of 1-2 mature individuals;
- A group of *Brachiosauruses* is considered a *colossal* target;
- Immature individuals don’t count towards the group limit;
- Grouped members can all defend each-other if attacked;
- Several groups can join into a herd, but can only defend grouped members if attacked. If one group in the herd is attacked, the rest of the herd should disperse and leave behind the attacked party.

## 🧩 Mixed grouping
- Can freely group with any *small* herbivores;
- **Jurassic Sheriff**: can group with *Kentrosaurus*, *Miragaia*, *Yunnanosaurus* and *Amargasaurus*.

## 🪺 Reproduction
- Nest materials: rock, mud;
- Prefer to dig out nests on dry sandy soil;
- Young *Brachiosauruses* are known as sauropodlets;
- Like most sauropods, *Brachiosauruses* do not exhibit much parental care;
- Young sauropodlets often travel in same-age groups called *creches*;
- Mature quickly, but growth gradually slows down at later life stages.


## 🌳 Claiming food
- *Brachiosaurus* has a very generalised palate and does not care for claiming berry bushes.

## 🚩 Claiming a territory
- *Brachiosaurus* can choose to claim any land POI as its *Park* by constructing a nest;
- While patrolling its *Park*, may challenge any *giant* or *colossal* creatures to leave the territory;
- If the nest of a *Brachiosaurus* is fully destroyed, the territory is considered lost, and cannot be reclaimed for at least another 15 minutes;
- Older individuals, especially males, tend to become increasingly more grumpy and territorial with age, chasing off and fighting all other sauropods and giant animals.

## 🪦 Other interactions
- A dead *Brachiosaurus* counts as a *Whalefall* and cannot be claimed by any one party. All carnivores may freely share the dead sauropod, and no conflicts can start on a whalefall.


## ☂️ Survival tips
- *Brachiosaurus* can consume almost anything. Food is not an issue;
- The biggest threat to a *Brachiosaurus* are giant Carcharodontosaurids that can slowly bleed out even a colossus. Good terrain usage is often a key to survial;
- Like any young sauropods, young *Brachiosauruses* are very vulnerable and should rely on hiding;
- Common nickname: brachi;
- Survival difficulty: ⭐️⭐️⭐️⭐️(challenging)
