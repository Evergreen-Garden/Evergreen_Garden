## 🐉 Speculative evolution
In real life there is no such thing as dragons. However, this doesn’t stop us from trying to imagine how such a creature may have evolved in the confines of known evolutionary rules. 
The clade *Vivernoidea*, or “Pseudodragons” is a group of highly derived squamates (lizards) with toxicoferan affinities. They started out as humble venomous tree lizards related to monitors, mosasaurs, snakes and gila monsters, but at some point became adapted for gliding, and eventually, flight. Their 5-fingered arms have evolved into membranous wings similar in structure to those of bats. Their base metabolism has become much faster, although pseudodragons are not as energetically efficient as pterosaurs and dinosaurs. Most pseudodragons retain venom, a forked tongue and a light kinetic skull typical for monitor lizards and their relatives. One aspect of monitor lizard physiology that has also got carried over may explain how pseudodragons took to the skies: monitors are known to have a highly efficient unidirectional airflow type of breathing, similar to dinosaurs and pterosaurs. This adaptation likely helped *vivernoids* in adopting this energy-demanding lifestyle.
Most of the *vivernoids* are small jungle fliers native to tropical islands, but a few members of the family *Pterophidae* have reached large size rivalling giant pterosaurs.
Pseudodragons are less efficient long-distance flyers than birds and pterosaurs, but excel at thermal soaring. Their long tails generate a lot of drag, but help them achieve great aerial maneuverability, aiding in rapidly changing direction.
Like most lizards, pseudodragons shed their skin.


## In the Garden:
*Echidrako* (Ancient Greek for “*viper dragon*”) is a large pseudodragon of the family *Pterophidae*. It is native to the coasts of Riparia. It was long regarded as a mere cryptid, a legend. Until now…
Colloquially known as “Dracovipers”, *Echidrako* are coastal aerial bandits. These flying lizards may lack the grace of pterosaurs, but they make up for it by being brazen and roguish. With a venomous bite and monitor lizard manners, *Echidrako* plunder the shorelines and mountains of Riparia.
The scientists of the Garden are intrigued to see how this unusual creature will make its stand in the ecosystem.


### ☁️ Habitat preference:
- **Coastal**: *Echidrako* can usually be found foraging on coastlines;
- **Montane**: return to roost at high cliffs after foraging at sea;
- **Caves**: *Echidraco* loves to make home in large caverns;
- **Elder trees**: giant landmark trees serve as points where *Echidraco* congregate, socialise and look for mates.

## 🌿 Roleplaying guidelines
- Enjoy sunning their wings in warm rays, occasionally squabbling over the best basking spots;
- Like many smaller pseudodragons, *Echidrako* retains semi-aquatic affinities;
- *Echidrako* uses its horns mainly for display and to intimidate rivals;
- Attract potential partners by spreading their wings;
- *Echidrako* has the ability to spit venom to blind its foes similarly to spitting cobras;
- Often roost together in large numbers. Each roost is guarded by tooth and claw, as space is limited!
- Can become quite hostile to one-another when competing for space, and even resort to cannibalism within the colony if there’s nothing else to hunt;
- Often sniff out and swarm corpses like vultures, making lots of noise in the process;
- Often practice kleptoparasitism.


## 💛 Grouping
- A group of 2-3 *Echidrako* is called a flight;
- A group of *Echidrako* is considered a *huge* target;
- Immature individuals don’t count towards the group limit;
- Grouped members can all defend each-other if attacked.

## 🧩 Mixed-species grouping
- Can freely group with *Rhamphorhynchus*.

## 🪺 Reproduction
- **Nest materials**: branches, reeds;
- Prefer to nest on high rocks and in caverns;
- **Colonial nesters**: build nests close together to provide safety in numbers. Mature individuals frequently return to where they hatched to raise their own drakelings;
- Young *Echidrako* are known as drakelings;
- *Echidrako* usually doesn’t give much parental care after helping its brood to hatch;
- Young drakelings still often follow their parent to scavenge on its kills;
- As a result of slower metabolism, grow much slower than similarly-sized pterosaurs;
- Exhibit linear growth, having the same growth speed throughout all of their life stages.


## 🍖 Hunting
- A lone *Echidrako* may hunt animals of up to *huge* size;
- A flight (2-3) can hunt targets of any size;
- **Infanticidal**: can hunt immature *Echidrako*;
- **Cannibalistic**: may hunt mature *Echidrako* when below 50% hunger.

## 🦴 Carcass interactions
- Can challenge any creature within hunting limits for a claimed carcass;
- Can freely feed from *Echidrako* carcasses;
- **Scavenger**: can eat or steal from claimed carcasses as long as the owner isn’t actively eating.


## ☂️ Survival tips
- Dracovipers can dive underwater to fish, and if that fails, you can always turn to scavenging beached carcasses;
- Venom spit only works if landed **on the head** of the target;
- Common nickname: draco;
- Survival difficulty: ⭐️⭐️ (not very hard)
