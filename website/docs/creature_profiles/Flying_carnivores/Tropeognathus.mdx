# Tropeognathus

## Known species
- *T. mesembrinus*;
- *T. magnirostris*;
- *T. robustus*;

**Size category**: medium;<br/>
**Diet**: fish, mollusks, meat, bones, arthropods;<br/>
**Supplementary food**: fruit;<br/>
**Nest materials**: reeds, branches;

## In real life
*Tropeognathus* was a large pterosaur from Early Cretaceous South America. It is the largest non-azhdarchid pterosaur known to science. It bore a distinctive keel on the tips of its jaws, which likely served display purposes. It was adapted to life at sea, soaring above the waves and catching slippery aquatic prey with its needle-like teeth.

## In the Garden
In the skies of Gondwa, *Tropeognathus* is an infamous pirate, a scoundrel and a thief. This opportunistic seafaring pterosaur is adapted to primarily eat fish, but will never turn away from a free meal, be it carrion or a lost juvenile dinosaur. Known for their mean temperament, these coastal pillagers are always looking for trouble.
There is, however, another side to this animal. Away from the noisy coasts and when not looking for food, they can quietly soar above the island with imperial majesty. There is nothing quite like seeing the flight of a *Tropeognathus*.

## Roleplaying guidelines
- Prefer to soar over coastal habitats and oceanic waters;
- Are highly opportunistic feeders, they will forage both on land and at sea with the majority of their food found near coastlines;
- Prefers to build nests on top of dramatic coastal cliffs and on islands;
- Colonial nesters: group members and even unrelated individuals are known to form noisy pterosaur bazaars, covering cliff faces with closely situated nests;
- Nesting pairs are known to form lifetime bonds and welcome each-other with a dance;
- Court each-other with aerial acrobatics;
- Can live to incredibly old age;
- When scavenging, will display their dominance over carcasses with an outstretched posture.


## Friendly interactions
- Can have up to 4 mature individuals in a group, called a squadron;
- Can have unlimited immature individuals in a group, called flaplings;
- Can group with *Rhamphorhynchus*, but many see them as nothing but a quick snack;
- A lone *Tropeognathus* can group with any aquatic carnivores for mutual aid in fishing and hunting. 

## Conflicts
- Lone *Tropeognathus* don’t hunt or challenge animals that exceed medium size, often targeting young dinosaurs;
- A squadron can hunt and challenge animals of up to large size;
- Some lone *Tropeognathus* readily challenge and battle others of their own kind to drive them off their territory;
- Are extremely aggressive and will kill other similarly sized pterosaurs;
- Squadrons often mob and bully azhdarchids; 
- Partial cannibals: can see unrelated flaplings as food;
- Often duel using leg tussles and wingbeats to knock each-other off cliffs;

## Other interactions
- Parents are known to knock their flaplings from the nest to give them a harsh first flying lesson;
- Can attempt to share a meal with random carnivores or steal meat chunks at their own risk, and eagerly do so;
- Communal feeders: can tolerate unrelated *Tropeognathus* descending and feeding at their kill;
- Often circle above active hunts like vultures to try and scavenge the fallen animal after the battle;
- Can engage in playful or mischievous behavior with other animals;
- **Scavenger**: can steal or eat from claimed carcasses as long as the owner isn’t actively eating.

## Survival tips
- Your low food and water drain allow you to undertake long extended journeys across the island;
- *Ramming rampage* does very little damage to grounded targets;
- *Tropeognathus* is a competent swimmer;
- You can rest and regain stamina on the surface of water;
- Common nicknames: trope, tropeo;
- Survival difficulty: ⭐️ (easy)


## Roleplaying inspirations
- Large birds of the high seas like the albatross, the giant petrel, the Antarctic skua and the frigatebird.
