# Kelenken

## Known species
- *K. guillermoi*;

## Related genera
- *Titanis walleri*;
- *Phorusrhacos longissimus*;

**Size category**: medium;<br/>
**Diet**: meat, fish, animal remains;<br/>
**Supplementary food**: fruits, roots;<br/>
**Nest materials**: reeds, branches, roots;

## In real life
*Kelenken* is an enormous flightless bird that lived in Argentina in the Miocene epoch. It belonged to the *Phorusrhacidae* family, also known as “The Terror Birds”, and was the largest known member. It was the apex predator that hunted  ancient mammals in open savanna with its giant hooked beak. Despite not being from the Mesozoic, it was as deadly as the dinosaurs of old.

## In the Garden
On the isle of Gondwa, *Kelenken*, despite being one of the largest birds to have ever existed, is dwarfed by the majority of predators. Forced to adapt, the terror birds have become supreme opportunists, hunting and thieving from under the noses of giant theropods and other primeval horrors. Bold and intelligent, *Kelenken* have equally quick wits and quick feet, stealing young from large dinosaurs and bullying predators from their kills. They are the feathered bandits of grasslands and savannas.


## Roleplaying guidelines
- Sexual dimorphism: male *Kelenken* often sport a crest of feathers on top of their head for display purposes, while most of the females don’t. This is not a hard rule, but merely a statistical curiosity. *Titanis* and *Phorucrhacos* don’t have sexually dimorphic features;
- Perfectly adapted for roaming vast open grasslands in search of food and accomplices;
- Prefer to nest on the ground in semi-open habitats like scrublands or the edges where forest meets grassland;
- Diurnal hunters that rely on their sharp vision. Tend to slow down at night and lose their daytime bravado a little;
- Use beak clacks to communicate with conspecifics;
- This species is known to sometimes form strong pair bonds, with mates regularly performing display for each-other and ritualistically gifting each-other dead critters. This bond transcends reproductive needs and can form between birds of any sex, and rarely even trios;
- Unrelated groups of *Kelenken* sometimes meet up and do dance-offs against each-other. 

## Friendly interactions
- Can have up to 3 mature individuals in a group, called a stride;
- Can have unlimited immature individuals in a group, called chicks;
- Parents exhibit great care of their clutch;
- Can group with *Rhamphorhynchus*;
- __Cannot__ group with deviant *Metriacanthosaurus* because the birds are too skeptical and mistrustful of the larger carnivore. The friend is *too dubious*.

## Conflicts
- Lone *Kelenken* can hunt  animals of up to large size;
- In the daytime groups of terror birds are bold enough to hunt anything they please, sometimes even springing ambushes on wounded or sleeping giant animals;
- In the night they aren’t as active and don’t target prey above large size;
- A stride can contest any carnivore for a carcass and love a free meal.


## Other interactions
- Can attempt to share a meal with random carnivores or steal meat chunks at their own risk, and eagerly do so;
- Communal feeders: can tolerate unrelated *Kelenken* feeding at their kill to butcher the carcass more efficiently;
- Often observe active hunts like vultures to try and steal a few bites of meat off the fallen animal after the battle;
- Often closely follow around herds of big herbivores to catch any critters getting spooked by the commotion; In such situations they often calm the herbivores with peaceful clacks and vocalisations to show that they are not a threat;
- If a chick dies, its parents and siblings can cannibalise the body. *Kelenken* do not hunt unrelated chicks.

## Survival tips
- You are speedy, but not very maneuverable! Avoid fighting in places where you can get stuck on terrain easily! 
- Common nickname: kel;
- Survival difficulty: ⭐️⭐️ (not very hard)
