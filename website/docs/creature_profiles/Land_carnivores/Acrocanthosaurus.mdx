## 🇺🇸 In real life
*Acrocanthosaurus* was a massive carnivorous theropod that roamed the whole of North America during the Early Cretaceous. It belonged to the lineage of *Charcharodontosauridae*, or the “shark-toothed predators”. The most prominent feature of *Acrocanthosaurus* were the tall neural spines that supported immensely strong spinal ligaments. It shared its habitat with the small raptor *Deinonychus*! This North American marauder most likely preyed upon the young of sauropods that were plentiful in its environment.

## In the Garden
The iconic *Acrocanthosaurus* is the tough rugged bandit of the Carcharodontosaur family. It may not be as gigantic as *Giganotosaurus* or *Tyrannotitan*, or as nimble as *Concavenator*, but it has the endurance and tenacity of a pitfighter, wearing down its prey in a struggle of attrition.
Utilising “dirty tactics”, it concusses opponents with brutal bashes, aims for vital arteries and even shoves its foes from tall cliffs, all the while disregarding pain and injury to its own hide. You wouldn’t want to cross paths with this gangster!


## ⛰️ Habitat preference
- **Coastal**: *Acrocanthosaurus* prefers to inhabit humid coastal forests;
- **Wetland**: often can be found stalking boggy landscapes and muddy marshes;
- **Woodland**: feels at home in tropical and monsoonal forests.

## 🌿 Roleplaying guidelines
- Highly migratory, covering great distances while roaming the coastlines and woodlands;
- Enjoys wallowing in wetland mud to take care of its scales;
- Greedy eaters;
- Counterintuitively, some individuals were observed slashing themselves with their own claws during aggressive display. This behaviour is poorly understood, but theorised to serve ritualistic purposes of intimidating rivals, a way of showing off toughness;
- Alternatively, some specialists see it as a parasite-induced behaviour, and the subsequent burst of rage as plain frustration with swamp ticks;
- Often select and ambush weak prey, like isolating juveniles from a herd;
- Often know when to retreat when faced with stronger predators: no territory is worth dying for;
- Atypically for large charcharodontosaurids, often form long-term nesting pairs that cohabit a territory, hunt and fend off competition together;
- When in a pair often bully larger members of their own family like *Giganotosaurus* and *Tyrannotitan* if they catch them being alone.


## ♥️ Grouping
- A group of 2 *Acrocanthosaurus* is called a pair;
- A group of *Acrocanthosaurus* is considered a *giant* target;
- Immature individuals don’t count towards the group limit;
- Grouped members can all defend each-other if attacked.

## 🧩 Mixed-species grouping
- Can freely group with *Rhamphorhynchus*. With such a tall body there’s plenty of space to hitch a ride!
- A lone *Acrocanthosaurus* may pair up with 1 *Squire Metriacanthosaurus* (see deviant profile).

## 🪺 Reproduction
- **Nest materials**: bones, rock, mud;
- Prefer to nest on boggy soil;
- Nests are often littered with discarded remains;
- Young *Acrocanthosaurus* are known as pickles;
- *Acrocanthosaurus* are good parents, but in most cases only one adult takes care of the brood;
- Mature quickly, but growth gradually slows down at later life stages.


## 🍖 Hunting
- A lone *Acrocanthosaurus* may hunt animals of any size;
- A pair (2) may hunt targets of *huge* size and above together. For smaller prey only one individual may engage;
- **Infanticidal**: can hunt immature *Acrocanthosaurus*, eliminating future competition.

## 🦴 Carcass interactions
- Can challenge any creature within hunting limits for a claimed carcass. Why hunt when you can steal?
- Can freely feed from *Acrocanthosaurus* carcasses.

## 🚩 Claiming a territory
- *Acrocanthosaurus* can choose to claim a land POI as its *Hideout* by constructing a nest;
- While patrolling the *Hideout*, *Acrocanthosaurus* may challenge any land carnivores within hunting limits to leave its territory;
- If the nest is fully destroyed, the territory is considered lost, and cannot be reclaimed for at least another 15 minutes.


## ☂️ Survival tips
- Many of your attacks damage *yourself* back as recoil: be careful not to get carried away!
- *Acrocanthosaurus* has a high demand for food and drink: always be on the move to look for food and water!
- Play dirty! Apply dizziness, bleed and knockback to get combat advantage;
- *Acrocanthosaurus* has a lot of swimming stamina;
- Common nickname: acro;
- Survival difficulty: ⭐️⭐️⭐️ (moderately challenging)
